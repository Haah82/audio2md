**1. 吧 (ba)**
It's a neutral tone, and it only appears at the end of a sentence. The function we're seeing today is to make a suggestion or proposal. 吧 (ba).
For example:
我们分手吧。(Wǒmen fēnshǒu ba.) - Let's break up. When a relationship doesn't work anymore, one of you has to say this. 我们分手吧。(Wǒmen fēnshǒu ba.)
Or another sentence:
你试试这个吧。(Nǐ shìshi zhè ge ba.) or 你试试这个吧 (Nǐ shìshi zhèige ba) in spoken language. It means: Try this. Like, I suggest you try this. A salesperson will say this to you: 你试试这个吧。(Nǐ shìshi zhè ge ba.)
Let's read the word again: 吧 (ba).

**2. 白 (bái)**
White. 白 (bái).
Like for example:
白雪公主 (Báixuě gōngzhǔ) - Snow White. Yes, the Disney princess. 白雪公主 (Báixuě gōngzhǔ).
Another example:
这件白裙子很漂亮。(Zhè jiàn bái qúnzi hěn piàoliang.) - This white dress is very beautiful. 这件白裙子很漂亮。(Zhè jiàn bái qúnzi hěn piàoliang.)
Let's read the word again: 白 (bái).

**3. 百 (bǎi)**
Hundred. 百 (bǎi).
Now it's a third tone, a low tone. When it's in a sentence, just try to make it as low as you can.
For example:
我爷爷一百岁了。(Wǒ yéye yībǎi suì le.) - My grandpa is 100 years old. 我爷爷一百岁了。(Wǒ yéye yībǎi suì le.)
Another example:
给我两百块钱。(Gěi wǒ liǎngbǎi kuài qián.) - Give me 200 kuai. 给我两百块钱。(Gěi wǒ liǎngbǎi kuài qián.)
Let's read the word again: 百 (bǎi).

Let me read again: 帮助 (bāngzhù).

**6. 比 (bǐ)**
Is a preposition. It means "than". We use that to make a comparison.
For example:

Another example:

**8. 长 (cháng)**
Means long. 长 (cháng).

Another example:
我喜欢你很长时间了。(Wǒ xǐhuan nǐ hěn cháng shíjiān le.) - I have liked you for a long time. You use this sentence to confess your love to someone: 我喜欢你很长时间了。(Wǒ xǐhuan nǐ hěn cháng shíjiān le.)

**9. 唱歌 (chànggē)**

**10. 出 (chū)**
It means "get out", get out of some place. Normally it's not used alone, you have to put a complement after it.
For example: 出来 (chūlái) - Come out. It's when you are out of the door, and you want the person inside the room to come out to the place you are, okay? 出来 (chūlái).
And 出去 (chūqù). Now you are inside the room with someone else, and you want that person to get out of the room, so it's 出去 (chūqù).
Can you read it? 出 (chū).

**12. 船 (chuán)**

**13. 次 (cì)**
It's a measure word, but not for nouns; instead it's for an action.
For example: 一次 (yī cì) - One time. 两次 (liǎng cì) - Twice. 三次 (sān cì) - Three times.
Let's see this sentence:
我去过两次长城。(Wǒ qùguo liǎng cì Chángchéng.) - I have been to the Great Wall twice.
Attention to the word order here: 我去过两次长城。(Wǒ qùguo liǎng cì Chángchéng.)
Let's read the word again: 次 (cì).

**14. 从 (cóng)**
It's a preposition, it means "from". From a place or from a time. 从 (cóng).
But in the following sentences, pay a lot of attention to the word order, see where it appears in the sentence:

Another example:
我从明天开始上班。(Wǒ cóng míngtiān kāishǐ shàngbān.) - I will start to work from tomorrow. Again, the literal order is: I from tomorrow start work. 我从明天开始上班。(Wǒ cóng míngtiān kāishǐ shàngbān.)
Can you read the word? 从 (cóng).

**20. 得 (de)**
It's a neutral tone, and it's a function word, well, a very important function word. It's used after a verb to introduce a complement of result or degree. Let me explain you with example sentences:
他跑得很快。(Tā pǎo de hěn kuài.) - He runs very fast. Here is the logic: 他 (tā) - he; 跑 (pǎo) - run. 他跑 (tā pǎo) - he runs. But how's his running? Well, very fast. So we put 得 (de) to introduce "fast". So 他跑得很快 (tā pǎo de hěn kuài) - he runs very fast.
Another example:
他睡得很早。(Tā shuì de hěn zǎo.) - He sleeps very early. 他 (tā) - he; 睡 (shuì) - sleep. 他睡 (tā shuì) - he sleeps. And we use 得 (de) to introduce "early". So he sleeps early: 他睡得很早 (tā shuì de hěn zǎo).

**24. 懂 (dǒng)**
It means understand. 懂 (dǒng).
It works in two ways. The first way is working just like a normal verb, like:
我懂了。(Wǒ dǒng le.) - I understand, after you explaining it to me. 我懂了。(Wǒ dǒng le.)
The second way is working as a complement after a verb. This is a very important grammar point, pay attention.
For example:
我听懂了。(Wǒ tīngdǒng le.) - I listened and I understood what you said. So here 懂 (dǒng), understand, is a complement of 听 (tīng), listen. 我听懂了。(Wǒ tīngdǒng le.)
Or:
我看懂了。(Wǒ kàndǒng le.) - It means I watched and I understood what I saw. Again, here 懂 (dǒng), understand, is a complement of the verb 看 (kàn), watch, see. 我看懂了。(Wǒ kàndǒng le.)
Let's read the word again: 懂 (dǒng).

**25. 对 (duì)**
We know that 对 (duì) means "right", "correct", to confirm the thing that another person said. But the meaning we're learning today is a preposition, it means "to" or "for". It's used before a noun or a pronoun, but the position it appears in a sentence is very different in Chinese, so pay attention.
For example:
他对我很好。(Tā duì wǒ hěn hǎo.) - He is very nice to me. Notice the word order here. The literal order is: He to me very nice. So the rule is: the subjects that involve come together in the beginning: 他对我 (Tā duì wǒ) - he to me. Then comes "very nice", 很好 (hěn hǎo). 他对我很好。(Tā duì wǒ hěn hǎo.)
Another example:
喝酒对身体不好。(Hē jiǔ duì shēntǐ bù hǎo.) - Drinking is bad for health. The literal order is: Drinking alcohol to health not good. 喝酒对身体不好。(Hē jiǔ duì shēntǐ bù hǎo.)
Let's read the word again: 对 (duì).

**32. 给 (gěi)**
It has two functions. The first function is being a normal verb, it means "give".
For example:

And the second function is being a preposition, it means "for". But pay attention to the position it appears in the sentence.
我给你买了一个礼物。(Wǒ gěi nǐ mǎi le yī ge lǐwù.) - I bought a gift for you. The literal order is: I for you bought a gift. So the people involved come together at the beginning of the sentence: 我给你 (wǒ gěi nǐ) - I for you. 我给你买了一个礼物。(Wǒ gěi nǐ mǎi le yī ge lǐwù.)
Can you read the word? 给 (gěi).

**37. 过 (guo)**
This is an very important function word, you're going to need it. It's used after a verb to indicate past experience. Let's see with example sentences:

Another sentence:

**38. 还 (hái)**
It has two main functions. The first one means "still".
For example:
九点了，他还在睡觉。(Jiǔ diǎn le, tā hái zài shuìjiào.) - It's 9 o'clock and he's still sleeping. 九点了，他还在睡觉。(Jiǔ diǎn le, tā hái zài shuìjiào.)
The second meaning is "possibly/barely satisfactory". It sounds hard to understand, but don't worry, you don't need to make sentences with it by yourself, because it only works with a couple of words, so you just need to remember those combinations.
For example, when someone ask your opinion of something, like "how is this book?", or "how's the food here?", if you say:
还可以 (hái kěyǐ) or 还不错 (hái búcuò) or 还行 (hái xíng) or 还好 (hái hǎo), all these mean not so bad, just passable, you're not totally satisfied with it, but it's okay.
Let's read them together:
还可以 (hái kěyǐ)
还不错 (hái búcuò)
还行 (hái xíng)

**64. 离 (lí)**
It means away. I'm afraid you will be totally confused without seeing example sentences. The thing is, we have to use this word in fixed structures. The structure we need to know in HSK 2 level is this one. It's to talk about distance.
The structure is: Location A + 离 (lí) + Location B, then you talk about the distance.
For example:
我家离公司很远。(Wǒ jiā lí gōngsī hěn yuǎn.) - I live far from the company. Literally: my home away from the company very far. So 我家 (wǒ jiā), my home, is the Location A. Then followed by 离 (lí). Later company, 公司 (gōngsī), is Location B. Then you talk about the distance: far, 很远 (hěn yuǎn).
Remember this formula when you need to talk about distance between two locations.
Another example:
学校离医院不远。(Xuéxiào lí yīyuàn bù yuǎn.) - The school is not far away from the hospital.
学校 (Xuéxiào) is Location A, and the hospital 医院 (yīyuàn) is Location B. They stay in the two sides of the word 离 (lí).

By the way, if you're confused with 有点 (yǒudiǎnr) and 一点 (yīdiǎnr), don't forget to check out one of my latest videos to see the explanation.
Now you read it.

**143. 丈夫 (zhàngfu)**
丈夫 (zhàngfu) - It means husband. But in real life speaking, 夫 (fu) just becomes a neutral tone. So 丈夫 (zhàngfu).
她丈夫比她高一点。(Tā zhàngfu bǐ tā gāo yīdiǎnr.)
她丈夫比她高一点。(Tā zhàngfu bǐ tā gāo yīdiǎnr.)
Her husband is a little taller than her.
她丈夫 (Tā zhàngfu) - we don't use 的 (de) with the people who are very close with us.
So literally: Her husband, than her, tall for a little bit.
Now you read it.

**144. 找 (zhǎo)**
找 (zhǎo) - To look for, to search for something.
我没找到喜欢的工作。(Wǒ méi zhǎodào xǐhuan de gōngzuò.)
我没找到喜欢的工作。(Wǒ méi zhǎodào xǐhuan de gōngzuò.)
I didn't find the job I like.
So 找 (zhǎo) is to look for, and 找到 (zhǎodào) is to find. So 到 (dào) is a complement to say the successful result of 找 (zhǎo).
Now you read it.

**145. 着 (zhe)**
着 (zhe) - An important particle. We use it to indicate a state, more like to indicate the continuation of a state.
他为什么穿着你的衣服？(Tā wèishénme chuānzhe nǐ de yīfu?)
他为什么穿着你的衣服？(Tā wèishénme chuānzhe nǐ de yīfu?)
Why is she wearing your clothes?
So 穿着 (chuānzhe) - wearing, is a continuous state.
Now you read it.

**146. 真 (zhēn)**
真 (zhēn) - It's an adverb, means really, indeed. Normally we use it to make an instant comment.
他唱得真好吃。(Tā chàng de zhēn hǎotīng.)
他唱得真好听。(Tā chàng de zhēn hǎotīng.)
He sings really well.
Remember, it's to do an instant comment on the things that you are experiencing in the current time. So she is singing now and you say: "Wow, she sings really well!"
Now you read it.

**147. 正在 (zhèngzài)**
正在 (zhèngzài) - It means in the process of. Let's see how to use it:
昨天你给我打电话的时候，我正在做饭。(Zuótiān nǐ gěi wǒ dǎ diànhuà de shíhou, wǒ zhèngzài zuòfàn.)
One more time:
昨天你给我打电话的时候，我正在做饭。(Zuótiān nǐ gěi wǒ dǎ diànhuà de shíhou, wǒ zhèngzài zuòfàn.)
So here you can use only 在 (zài), but if we use 正在 (zhèngzài), it emphasizes more that the process was going on in that certain specific time.
Now your turn, read it, please.

**148. 知道 (zhīdào)**
知道 (zhīdào) - To know.
你知道这本书是谁写的吗？(Nǐ zhīdào zhè běn shū shì shéi xiě de ma?)
你知道这本书是谁写的吗？(Nǐ zhīdào zhè běn shū shì shéi xiě de ma?)
Do you know who wrote this book?
So 是 (shì)...的 (de) is a fixed structure to say the doer of the action, the person who made the action.
Now you read it.

**149. 准备 (zhǔnbèi)**
准备 (zhǔnbèi) - To prepare.
明天的考试，你准备好了吗？(Míngtiān de kǎoshì, nǐ zhǔnbèi hǎole ma?)
明天的考试，你准备好了吗？(Míngtiān de kǎoshì, nǐ zhǔnbèi hǎole ma?)
Are you prepared for tomorrow's exam?
Attention: the subject in this sentence is 明天的考试 (míngtiān de kǎoshì), tomorrow's exam. That's the target, that's the topic we are talking about. Then: 你准备好了吗？(nǐ zhǔnbèi hǎole ma?) - Are you prepared?
So 准备 (zhǔnbèi) is prepare, and 准备好 (zhǔnbèi hǎo) is prepared, ready. So again, 好 (hǎo) is a complement to say a successful result of the action.
Now your turn, read it.

**150. 走 (zǒu)**
走 (zǒu) - To walk.
很近，走五分钟就到了。(Hěn jìn, zǒu wǔ fēnzhōng jiù dào le.)
很近，走五分钟就到了。(Hěn jìn, zǒu wǔ fēnzhōng jiù dào le.)
It's very near. You can walk there in 5 minutes.
You see 很近 (hěn jìn), there isn't a subject. It's "very near". And here 就 (jiù), remember this function word? We use it to mean something happens very fast or very soon. So in this sentence, it means it's very near, only need 5 minutes.
Now you read it.

**151. 最 (zuì)**
最 (zuì) - It means most, to the greatest extent.
我不知道哪个最好。(Wǒ bù zhīdào nǎge zuì hǎo.)
我不知道哪个最好。(Wǒ bù zhīdào nǎge zuì hǎo.)
I don't know which one is the best.
Now your turn.

**152. 左边 (zuǒbiān)**
左边 (zuǒbiān) - Left side.
他从左边走到了右边。(Tā cóng zuǒbiān zǒu dào le yòu biān.)
他从左边走到了右边。(Tā cóng zuǒbiān zǒu dào le yòu biān.)
He walked from the left side to the right side.
从 (Cóng) is "from", and 到 (dào) is "to".
Now you read it.
